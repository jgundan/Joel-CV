# About Me

I'm Joel Gundan, an Infrastructure Developer and IAM Security Engineer based in Rodriguez, Rizal, Philippines. For the past four years or so I've worked on the systems that decide who can get into what: hybrid Active Directory, Microsoft Entra ID, enterprise PKI, and the messaging and automation that sit around them.

## What I do

Most of my work is in identity and infrastructure. I design, deploy, and harden hybrid Active Directory environments, including domain controllers, Group Policy, replication, and the services that connect on-premises directories to Entra ID. I've managed certificate lifecycles with Venafi, administered Exchange Online and Microsoft 365 through the Microsoft Graph API and PowerShell, and handled second- and third-level support for global users.

I also like to automate things. Most of the repetitive work I've come across, like provisioning, mailbox and licensing operations, scheduled-task monitoring, and access reviews, is better as code. I write PowerShell and Python, build GitLab CI/CD pipelines, and use Terraform and Ansible where they fit.

## Where I've worked

- **ProV International** (February 2026 to present): Infrastructure Developer on DevOps and GitOps. I maintain hybrid AD infrastructure, automate Microsoft 365 operations, and build the pipelines and monitoring around them.
- **Wrist Ship Supply** (September 2024 to February 2026): IAM and IDaM Architect. I designed and ran hybrid identity for thousands of users across several countries, delivered SSO and MFA with SAML, OAuth 2.0, and OIDC, and automated joiner-mover-leaver workflows.
- **DXC Technology** (July 2022 to September 2024): Security Engineer for Identity and Access Management. I ran enterprise PKI with Venafi and worked on Active Directory, RSA SecurID, CA-PAM, and Azure IaaS hardening. I also trained people in PowerShell and built Power Automate workflows.

## How I work

I like systems that are documented, tested, and easy to hand over. I'd rather spend time making a process repeatable than fixing the same problem twice. When something breaks at 2 a.m., I want to know what failed, why, and how to stop it from happening again.
